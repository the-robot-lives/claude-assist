# Dara Lim — Minimart Modernizer

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 30-40 |
| **Role** | Owner, single minimart with 2-3 staff |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | Convenience retail / minimart |
| **Location** | Provincial town or Phnom Penh residential neighborhood |

## Bio

Dara opened his minimart four years ago after working in Thailand for a chain convenience store, where he saw how a real POS system kept staff honest and stock predictable. He runs a cheap Android tablet propped on the counter as his register today, using a mix of a spreadsheet app and a generic invoicing app that doesn't talk to each other. He is comfortable with technology but frustrated that nothing on the market is built for how Cambodian minimarts actually operate.

## Goals
1. Stop losing money to shrinkage he can't explain — cash or stock going missing between shifts.
2. See real-time stock levels so he isn't caught out of stock on fast movers like drinks and snacks.
3. Give his 2-3 staff limited permissions so they can sell but not silently discount or void.

## Frustrations
1. His current tablet setup doesn't reconcile a shift's cash drawer against sales automatically.
2. He suspects shrinkage but has no audit trail to point to a specific person or moment.
3. Restocking decisions are guesswork — he over-orders slow items and runs out of fast ones.
4. Existing POS apps he's tried are either in English only or priced for large retail chains.

## Behaviors
- Runs the register himself on busy shifts, delegates to staff on slower ones.
- Currently tracks inventory in a spreadsheet he updates inconsistently, usually falling behind.
- Pays suppliers in a mix of cash and bank transfer, tracks debts to them loosely.
- Checks his phone frequently during the day even when not at the shop, wants remote visibility.

## Job to Be Done
> "When I'm not physically at the shop, I want to see what's selling, what's low, and whether the drawer matches the sales log, so I can trust my staff without having to watch over them."

## Relationship to Product
Discovery likely comes from actively searching for "POS Cambodia" or a recommendation in a small-business Facebook group — he is a motivated researcher, not a passive adopter. He will evaluate the product against the theft-control and stock-visibility promise specifically, testing the audit trail and staff permissions before committing real inventory to the app. Key adoption features: cash register balance/reconciliation, per-staff-member audit trail, and low-stock alerts; forecasting is a strong secondary draw once the basics prove reliable. Churn risk: if reconciliation reports are unclear, if the app doesn't work reliably offline during Cambodia's frequent brief outages, or if staff find workarounds that defeat the audit trail's purpose.

## Scenarios
1. **Shift handover reconciliation** — At shift change, Dara wants to see expected-vs-actual cash for the outgoing staff member's shift, flagged automatically if there's a discrepancy above a threshold.
2. **Remote stock check** — While running errands, Dara opens the app on his phone to see which items are below reorder threshold and messages his supplier before heading back.
