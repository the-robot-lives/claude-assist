# Vanna Sok — Front-Counter Cashier

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 18-24 |
| **Role** | Employed cashier, minimart or small shop |
| **Technical Level** | Novice-to-intermediate, but smartphone-native |
| **Industry** | Retail (employee, not owner) |
| **Location** | Phnom Penh or provincial town minimart |

## Bio

Vanna is a university student working part-time behind the counter to help pay tuition. She grew up with a smartphone in her hand and can navigate any app icon-first without reading instructions, but she has zero patience for anything that makes a simple task take more taps than it should. She didn't choose the POS system her employer uses — she just has to get through her shift fast and not get blamed for someone else's mistake.

## Goals
1. Ring up a sale as fast as physically possible, especially during rush periods.
2. Prove her shift's cash and sales were accurate so she isn't blamed for shortfalls she didn't cause.
3. Learn the system in minutes on her first shift with no formal training.

## Frustrations
1. Any POS that requires more than 2-3 taps to complete a common sale feels broken to her.
2. Ambiguous UI icons or English jargon slow her down when a customer is waiting.
3. Being held responsible for drawer discrepancies from a previous shift she didn't work.
4. Managers who don't clearly show her exactly what she's accountable for at shift start/end.

## Behaviors
- Swipes and taps through apps intuitively, expects instant visual feedback on every action.
- Uses her personal phone for everything — Telegram, TikTok, mobile banking — comfortable with QR payments.
- Wants a visible timestamp/log tying actions to her login so she has proof of her own shift's activity.
- Will find and exploit any shortcut or workaround if the "correct" flow is too slow.

## Job to Be Done
> "When I start and end my shift, I want the system to clearly log what happened under my login, so I can move fast during the shift and not get blamed for something I didn't do."

## Relationship to Product
Vanna never chooses the product — she's onboarded by her employer (Dara Lim, P-002, or a manager) and forms her opinion in the first five minutes of her first shift. Adoption is really about not-rejection: if the sell screen is fast and the login/logout shift boundary is unambiguous, she'll use it without complaint; if it's slow or confusing, she'll pressure her employer to go back to the old way or will work around it. The features she actually touches are the sell screen, KHQR/cash tender toggle, and shift open/close — she never sees forecasting or multi-store reporting. Churn risk (for the business, via her): a clunky checkout flow that makes her look slow to customers, or being blamed for reconciliation issues she can't self-verify.

## Scenarios
1. **Rush-hour checkout** — A line of five customers forms; Vanna needs to scan/ring items, apply KHQR or cash tender, and print or send a receipt in seconds per transaction.
2. **Shift-end handover** — At the end of her shift she wants to see her own sales total and cash count clearly separated from the next cashier's, so any discrepancy investigation doesn't default to blaming her.
