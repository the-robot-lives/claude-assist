# Marie Dubois — NGO Program Enabler

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 30-45 |
| **Role** | Program manager, livelihoods NGO / merchant co-op |
| **Technical Level** | Intermediate |
| **Industry** | International development / livelihoods NGO |
| **Location** | Based in Phnom Penh, program sites across rural/peri-urban Cambodia |

## Bio

Marie runs a livelihoods program that helps informal-sector merchants — market vendors and small shopkeepers — formalize their businesses and improve financial resilience. Digitizing member merchants' record-keeping is a program goal tied to donor reporting requirements, but her member base spans dozens to hundreds of merchants with wildly varying literacy, tech comfort, and connectivity. She needs a tool she can roll out at scale with training materials her field staff (not her) will actually deliver.

## Goals
1. Onboard a batch of merchants (tens to hundreds) into the system efficiently, not one at a time.
2. Provide training materials her local field staff can use to teach merchants with minimal tech background.
3. Report aggregate program outcomes (adoption rate, sales growth, formalization indicators) to donors.
4. Ensure the tool works reliably for merchants in areas with poor connectivity and inconsistent power.

## Frustrations
1. Most software isn't designed for bulk onboarding — she'd have to set up each merchant manually.
2. Existing training materials for similar tools are in English or written for a more tech-literate user than her members.
3. Donor reporting cycles demand aggregate data she currently has to collect manually via field visits.
4. Poor rural connectivity means anything requiring constant sync fails her program's most vulnerable members.

## Behaviors
- Coordinates a network of field staff who do the actual in-person merchant training and support.
- Reports program metrics to donors on a quarterly or semi-annual cycle.
- Evaluates tools partly on merit, partly on donor/partner requirements around data and accountability.
- Prioritizes accessibility and inclusivity (literacy level, disability, gender) in program tool choices.

## Job to Be Done
> "When I'm rolling out a digital record-keeping tool to a cohort of member merchants, I want simple bulk onboarding and training materials my field staff can use, so I can scale the program without a 1:1 support burden and still report outcomes to donors."

## Relationship to Product
Marie discovers the product through outreach from the vendor targeting NGO/development partnerships, or by researching options to fulfill a program's digitization mandate — she is an institutional buyer/champion, not an end user of the register herself. Her adoption criteria center on features individual merchants never touch: bulk account provisioning, exportable training material in Khmer, and offline resilience robust enough for program sites with unreliable power and connectivity, since her members are often in more marginal conditions than urban shop owners like Dara Lim. She may also need lightweight aggregate reporting across her whole cohort for donor reports, distinct from Chenda's commercial multi-store dashboard. Churn risk: if onboarding still requires per-merchant manual setup at any meaningful scale, or if offline behavior isn't resilient enough for her program's most under-resourced sites, undermining the program's own digitization promise to donors.

## Scenarios
1. **Cohort rollout** — Marie's team onboards 40 member merchants from a single district in one batch ahead of a training workshop, using bulk setup rather than configuring each merchant's shop individually.
2. **Field training day** — A field officer runs a hands-on training session with a group of merchants using Marie's prepared Khmer-language materials, demonstrating the app on a shared tablet in an area with intermittent connectivity.
