# Sokha Chea — Market-Stall Matriarch

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 45-55 |
| **Role** | Owner-operator, single market stall |
| **Technical Level** | Novice |
| **Industry** | Fresh produce / dry goods, wet market retail |
| **Location** | Phnom Penh wet market (e.g. Orussey, Chbar Ampov) |

## Bio

Sokha has run her stall for over twenty years, inheriting the space and the trade from her own mother. She keeps a paper exercise book under the scale for tallying sales and owed credit to regular customers, and does the day's math in her head or on a pocket calculator at closing. Her only computing device is a mid-range Android phone she uses for Telegram with her children and to check exchange rates.

## Goals
1. Know at the end of each day whether she made money or lost money, without doing arithmetic by hand.
2. Keep track of who owes her credit (many regulars buy on tab) without losing the paper book.
3. Avoid anything that slows down a sale — customers are impatient and stalls beside her are faster.

## Frustrations
1. Her paper ledger gets smudged, torn, or occasionally "borrowed" and not returned.
2. She has been shortchanged by hurried mental math during peak morning rush.
3. She distrusts apps in English — anything not in Khmer script is unusable to her.
4. She has heard of Bakong/KHQR but doesn't understand how it reconciles with cash at day's end.

## Behaviors
- Tallies sales with tick marks in a notebook, totals with a plastic calculator.
- Tracks customer credit ("borrow book") by writing names in Khmer she recognizes on sight.
- Prices everything in Cambodian Riel, occasionally quotes USD for larger baskets from tourists.
- Relies on her adult daughter to help her with anything involving a screen.

## Job to Be Done
> "When I close my stall at the end of a market day, I want to see instantly how much I sold and who still owes me, so I can go home without doing the sums myself and worrying I got it wrong."

## Relationship to Product
Discovery is almost certainly word-of-mouth through her daughter or a neighboring stall owner who has already adopted it — she will not find or install the app herself. Adoption hinges entirely on the Khmer-first UI and a sell screen simple enough to use with zero training; if it requires reading instructions, she abandons it within a day. The credit/tab tracking and end-of-day total are the features that will keep her, not inventory or forecasting, which are too abstract for her current mental model of the business. Churn risk: any update that adds English-only text, a confusing new screen, or requires network connectivity she doesn't have at her stall.

## Scenarios
1. **Morning rush credit sale** — A regular customer buys vegetables on credit during a crowded 7am rush; Sokha needs to log the amount against the customer's name in two taps without breaking her flow serving the next person.
2. **End-of-day cash-up** — At 6pm she closes the stall and wants a single Khmer-language screen showing total sales, total credit outstanding, and cash counted, so she can walk home confident.
