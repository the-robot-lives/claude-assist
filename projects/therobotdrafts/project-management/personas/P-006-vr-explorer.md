# Aiko Tanaka — VR/XR Power User

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 27-36 |
| **Role** | Staff Engineer & XR hobbyist |
| **Technical Level** | Expert |
| **Industry** | Game / simulation tooling |
| **Location** | Remote, Osaka |

## Bio

Aiko owns a Quest 3 and an Index and genuinely thinks in 3D. She believes a codebase is a *space* and wants to literally walk through it. She's allergic to VR experiences that induce nausea or strand you in a mode with no way out.

## Goals

1. Explore large systems immersively at comfortable frame rates
2. Author and connect elements in VR with the same power as desktop
3. Switch fluidly between headset and flat-screen without losing her place

## Frustrations

1. Most "VR dev tools" are tech demos with one input and no authoring
2. Tiny targets and raw ray-picking make precise actions miserable
3. Motion sickness from world-locked menus and big camera jumps

## Behaviors

- Uses radial/wrist menus daily in other XR apps
- Notices frame drops instantly
- Expects desktop↔VR parity, not a crippled VR mode

## Job to Be Done

> "When I put on a headset, I want to fly through and edit a real system as comfortably as I navigate any polished XR app, so the spatial metaphor pays off instead of making me sick."

## Relationship to Product

Aiko is the VR-first validator. She values the radial menu, near-field keyboard, ray-pick assists, comfort tuning, and 90 fps on large graphs. She churns on nausea, missing authoring parity, or unreadable labels in-headset.

## Scenarios

1. **Headset fly-through** — Aiko loads a system in VR and uses the wrist radial to drill, focus, and connect nodes without ever touching a keyboard.
2. **Parity check** — She starts a diagram on desktop, picks it up in VR, and finishes authoring it there.
