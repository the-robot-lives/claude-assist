# Priya Raman — Tech Lead / Engineering Manager

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 33-42 |
| **Role** | Tech Lead, 8-person product team |
| **Technical Level** | Advanced |
| **Industry** | Healthcare software |
| **Location** | On-site, Toronto |

## Bio

Priya splits her time between code and people. She runs design reviews, defends refactors to product, and needs to communicate structure to engineers and non-engineers in the same meeting. She cares less about authoring perfect UML and more about a shared, current picture the team can point at.

## Goals

1. Run architecture and refactor reviews against a live, shared model
2. Communicate system structure to mixed technical/non-technical audiences
3. Plan refactors by seeing impact and dependencies before committing

## Frustrations

1. Reviews stall on "is this diagram still accurate?"
2. No common artifact the whole team trusts
3. Hard to show *why* a refactor is needed without a visual

## Behaviors

- Schedules recurring architecture syncs
- Screenshares diagrams that she's not confident are current
- Mentors juniors by walking them through the system

## Job to Be Done

> "When I lead a design review, I want to pull up a live model everyone can navigate together, so we argue about the real system instead of about whether the diagram is stale."

## Relationship to Product

Priya is a collaboration and review user. She values projection-to-standard-diagrams, annotation, sharing, and presentation-friendly views. She churns if she can't easily share or present what she finds, or if it needs an engineering setup ritual every time.

## Scenarios

1. **Refactor pitch** — Priya highlights a tangled region, annotates the cycles, and shares the view with product to justify a refactor.
2. **Onboarding walkthrough** — She gives a new hire a guided fly-through of the system's main subsystems.
