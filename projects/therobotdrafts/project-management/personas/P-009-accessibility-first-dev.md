# Theo Brandt — Accessibility-Constrained Developer

## Demographics

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Age** | 29-45 |
| **Role** | Senior Engineer |
| **Technical Level** | Expert |
| **Industry** | Civic tech |
| **Location** | Remote, Berlin |

## Bio

Theo is a strong engineer with deuteranopia (red-green colorblindness) and vestibular motion sensitivity that rules out VR and fast camera motion. He's used to tools that assume color-coding and smooth 3D flight are universal. He needs the same comprehension power through keyboard, high-contrast, monochrome-safe, and reduced-motion paths.

## Goals

1. Comprehend system structure without relying on color or VR
2. Drive the whole tool from the keyboard with reduced motion
3. Read labels and distinctions reliably regardless of hue

## Frustrations

1. Color-only encodings (red=this, green=that) are invisible to him
2. Smooth/fast 3D camera motion triggers nausea
3. Mouse-only, hover-dependent UIs lock him out of features

## Behaviors

- Runs OS-level high-contrast and reduce-motion settings
- Keyboard-navigates everything possible
- Verifies tools work in grayscale before committing to them

## Job to Be Done

> "When I use a spatial code tool, I want every distinction encoded beyond color and every action reachable by keyboard with motion I can tolerate, so my disability doesn't cost me the tool's value."

## Relationship to Product

Theo is the underserved accessibility persona the design's redundancy contract and reduced-motion goals exist for. He values monochrome-safe encoding (shape + label, not just hue), full keyboard control, reduced-motion options, and high-contrast/legible labels. He churns the moment a critical distinction is color-only or a key action is hover/VR-only.

## Scenarios

1. **Grayscale check** — Theo turns on monochrome mode and confirms he can still tell every node kind and edge type apart.
2. **Keyboard authoring** — He creates, connects, and edits elements entirely from the keyboard with reduced-motion camera transitions.
