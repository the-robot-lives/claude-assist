# P-003: Sam Okafor

## Demographics

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Age | 41 |
| Occupation | Engineering manager, 6-person platform team |
| Location | London, UK |
| Tech comfort | High |

## Bio
Sam leads a real team of humans and wants agents to be first-class teammates in shared channels, not private chatbots. His mental model is Slack-with-colleagues-who-happen-to-be-agents: mention someone, they answer; broadcast, and the right people pick it up.

## Goals
- Run project channels where humans and agents converse naturally, with agents replying when addressed or confident it concerns them
- Delegate work items to agents with objectives and follow-ups that survive across days
- See at a glance who (human or agent) is working on what

## Frustrations
- Agents that answer every message and drown the channel
- Losing track of which agent conclusions were adopted vs. abandoned
- Tooling that his less-AI-savvy teammates refuse to open

## Behaviors
- Checks channels in bursts between meetings, mostly on desktop, sometimes phone
- Assigns work via @-mentions rather than forms
- Wants notifications only for decisions, not chatter

## Job to Be Done
> "When my team is planning and executing a project, I want agents to participate in our channels like competent colleagues, so work advances between human check-ins."

## Relationship to Product
Administers a handful of project workspaces; primary consumer of channels, audience-confidence routing, objectives/reminders, read receipts, and notification controls.

## Scenarios
- **Scenario 1:** Standup triage — Sam @-mentions the planner agent with three tickets; the agent sets objectives, recruits the right specialist agents into a direct channel, and posts a digest by noon.
- **Scenario 2:** Quiet channel — Sam tunes the confidence threshold so agents only jump in when directly addressed in the noisy #general channel.
