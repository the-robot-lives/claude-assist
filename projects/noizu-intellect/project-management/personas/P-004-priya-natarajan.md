# P-004: Priya Natarajan

## Demographics

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Age | 37 |
| Occupation | Senior product manager |
| Location | Toronto, Canada |
| Tech comfort | Medium |

## Bio
Priya doesn't write prompts or code — she judges outcomes. In the parallel-path model she is the human in the loop: the person the system surfaces the top-K results to, whose pick becomes the reward signal. She needs comparison to be effortless and jargon-free.

## Goals
- Compare competing path outcomes side-by-side in plain language with clear trade-offs
- Pick a winner (or reject all) quickly and trust the system learns from her choice
- Track what happened to her past decisions

## Frustrations
- Being shown raw agent transcripts instead of summarized, comparable outcomes
- Not knowing whether her pick actually changed anything
- Interfaces that assume she knows what a "temperature" or "fork" is

## Behaviors
- Reviews in short sessions, often on a tablet
- Skims summaries first, drills into detail only for finalists
- Annotates decisions with a sentence of rationale for the team

## Job to Be Done
> "When agents produce several candidate solutions, I want to compare and pick the best in minutes, so the team ships the right option and the system gets better at proposing them."

## Relationship to Product
Interacts almost exclusively with the review/grade/pick surface: outcome comparison, ranked shortlists, pick-with-rationale, and decision history.

## Scenarios
- **Scenario 1:** Five-outcome review — Priya opens a review request, sees five graded outcome cards with a reviewer agent's scores, expands two, picks one, writes one line of rationale, done in eight minutes.
- **Scenario 2:** Retro — a month later Priya pulls up her decision history to show which picked path shipped and how the planner's proposals have shifted since.
