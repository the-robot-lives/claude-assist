# Carpet Grand Prix — Player Journey: P-002 (Sarah Chen)

**Persona:** The Micro-Gamer
**Focus:** Short windows around parenting, story-forward, low mechanical investment

## First-session journey

| Step | What she does | What she feels | Design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | Sees a friend's recommendation, or the free demo listed in a "games for kids to watch" roundup | Cautious interest — she has been burned by energy-gated free games before | The demo is a genuinely full slice (4 courses, full tilt system), not a crippled trial |
| 2. Install & purchase hesitation | Plays the free demo first, with her kids next to her on the couch | Testing the water — "is this going to nag me to pay?" | No IAP prompts anywhere in the demo; the only ask is the one-time $4.99 unlock screen after Playroom is cleared |
| 3. Purchase | Buys the full game after her kids ask to keep playing | Small guilt-free luxury purchase | Premium-once framing (`../design/monetization/revenue-model.md`) — no recurring ask to second-guess later |
| 4. First real session, kids watching | Plays with the phone held so her kids can see the screen, narrating the runs out loud | Delight at the toys-come-alive premise; the racing itself is secondary | The wordless vignettes unlock per room specifically so a room clear has a payoff a five-year-old can also read |
| 5. Interruption | A kid needs something mid-run; she sets the phone down | Confidence the game will not punish her for stopping | No crash-out failure model means an abandoned run just ends, no penalty beyond the missed medal |
| 6. Resume later that day | Picks the same course back up in a 4-minute window before dinner | Low cognitive re-entry cost | Calibration is instant (2s); there is no accumulated state to remember |
| 7. Story progression | Clears Kitchen and Hallway over several sittings, watching each vignette | Emotional engagement with the wordless story — "it's kind of sad, actually" | The narrative's melancholy-under-playful tone (see world design docs) lands specifically with a parent audience |
| 8. Hits the Basement | Tries a Basement course, finds the open-edge hazard and darkness genuinely stressful for a casual session | Mild frustration, decides it's not worth the effort | She is not the target audience for Sprue-tier mastery; the game does not gate the story behind Basement golds |

## What retains her

The **story, not the driving**, is her retention hook. Each room's audiovisual identity
shift (`../design/mechanics/difficulty-progression.md` world docs) gives her a reason to
keep progressing even at bronze-only skill, because the reward for reaching a new room is
narrative and atmospheric, not just mechanical. She is the persona most likely to
purchase The Garage Sale expansion, specifically because she wants more of the *house* and
its story, not more difficulty.

## Where she drops off

She finishes the main story but skips the harder golds in the Basement and Garden
entirely — she never returns to a completed room to grind medals. Her natural stopping
point is "story complete," which the design treats as a full, satisfying ending state
rather than a 60–70% completion to be chased further.
