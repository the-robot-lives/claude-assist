# Carpet Grand Prix — Player Journey: P-011 (Maria Rodriguez)

**Persona:** The Commuter Gamer
**Focus:** Dead-time reclamation, low commitment, offline-first

## First-session journey

| Step | What she does | What she feels | Design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | Sees the parallax effect in a short-form video clip while scrolling on her actual commute | Curiosity — "that looks like a real toy, not a screen" | The clip is the marketing asset by design (`../design/monetization/revenue-model.md`) |
| 2. Install | Downloads the free demo while standing on the platform, train not yet arrived | Low-risk — no payment screen, no account | Free demo has zero friction: no login, no network check |
| 3. First launch | Opens the app seated on the train, phone held low and slightly angled against her leg, not upright | Slight self-consciousness about tilting a phone in public | Calibration step reads *that* posture as neutral — she never has to hold the phone "correctly" |
| 4. Run 1 | Plays the Playroom's first course, over-tilts twice, bumps two rails, finishes anyway | Mild surprise that a mistake didn't end the run | Failure philosophy (time cost, never crash-out) absorbs her rough first attempt |
| 5. Sensor drift detected | The train pulls out of the station; the car begins drifting sideways on a straight | Confusion — "why is it pulling?" | After the second run showing sustained low-frequency drift, the game offers Vehicle Mode unprompted |
| 6. Vehicle Mode accepted | Taps the one-line prompt, the drift disappears on the next run | Relief, then a small "oh, nice" | This is the single moment that converts her from a one-and-done install to a returning player |
| 7. Three runs, phone away | Plays two more courses before her stop, gets a bronze on the third | Satisfaction, no urge to keep playing past her stop | Session length matches her actual travel window; nothing punishes stopping |
| 8. Next day | Opens the same course again on the way in | Habit forming — "a bit better than yesterday" | Ghost of her own best run gives her something to beat without any pressure to chase gold |

## What retains her

The between-session hook is entirely **her own ghost**, not the story or the medal
ladder — she is not chasing gold, she is chasing "slightly faster than yesterday's
commute." Vehicle Mode being discovered *for* her rather than requiring her to find a
settings menu is the single highest-leverage retention moment in her journey; without it,
train-motion drift would read as a broken game rather than a filterable one.

## Where she drops off

She stalls around Room 3 (Hallway) and rarely goes further. The story vignettes past that
point are content she will never see, and the game does not attempt to pull her forward
— completion is not her goal, and the design deliberately treats "complete at any medal
count" as a legitimate end state rather than a churn signal to fight. Her risk of
uninstalling is highest not at difficulty spikes but at any change to her commute (a new
job, a different train line) that removes the dead-time slot the game was filling.
