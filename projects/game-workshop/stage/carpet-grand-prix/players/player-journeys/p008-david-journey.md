# Carpet Grand Prix — Player Journey: P-008 (David Park)

**Persona:** The Achievement Hunter
**Focus:** Mastery, completion percentage, precision over time spent

## First-session journey

| Step | What he does | What he feels | Design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | Reads a review or forum post specifically about the tilt input's skill ceiling | Skepticism — most "tilt controls" he has tried are gimmicks | The free demo is his verification step before he trusts the review |
| 2. First run | Deliberately over-tilts to find the response curve's limits within the first course | Analytical interest — mapping the input space | ±32° full-lock and the α = 0.16 low-pass are tuned to feel consistent under exactly this kind of probing |
| 3. Notices the parallax cue | Within twenty minutes, realises the deck's height above the carpet telegraphs upcoming grade | The "aha" moment — this is the mechanic he is actually buying | Grade legibility (`../design/mechanics/primary-mechanic.md`, Output 3) is the entire point of the tilt-diorama design |
| 4. Purchases immediately | Buys the full $4.99 game before finishing the demo's four courses | High intent, no hesitation | Premium pricing with no IAP reads to him as a signal of a complete, non-exploitative design |
| 5. First ghost run | Beats his own bronze time, watches his ghost render at the correct diorama height on an elevated section | Excitement — this is data, not just a replay | Ghosts store raw tilt, not just position, specifically for players like him |
| 6. Systematic grinding begins | Starts clearing rooms in order, chasing gold on every course before moving to the next room | Methodical satisfaction | The medal ladder (`../design/meta-loop.md`) gives him a legible, non-RNG completion target |
| 7. Hits Sprue times | On the Basement's harder courses, discovers Sprue times require pre-loading tilt for grade changes he can't yet see | Frustration, then obsession | This is the third mastery layer ("read the height") by design — the ceiling is meant to bite exactly here |
| 8. Files a bug report | Notices inconsistent sensor behaviour on his specific handset and writes a detailed, reproducible report | Ownership over the game's quality, not just its content | Per-device sensor profiling (see platform technical docs) exists precisely because players like David surface OEM-specific drift issues |
| 9. 100% completion | Reaches all 38 golds plus a majority of Sprue times around the 11-hour mark | Pride, immediately followed by wanting more | He is the first to request a track editor — which ships free at month 6 |

## What retains him

**Ghost deltas in fractions of a second.** He is not chasing story or new rooms — he is
chasing his own line, shaved down in 0.05s increments, using the raw-tilt data stored
alongside every ghost. The single completion percentage visible from the main menu is his
primary north star; he checks it the way other players check a to-do list.

## Where he drops off

He does not drop off in the conventional sense — he 100%s the base game and then goes
quiet until new content ships. His retention risk is entirely about **content cadence**:
without the free track editor update at month 6 and The Garage Sale expansion at month 9,
he has nothing left to chase after roughly 11 hours, and he is vocal about wanting more
the moment he finishes.
