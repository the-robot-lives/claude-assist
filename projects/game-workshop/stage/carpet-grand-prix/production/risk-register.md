# Carpet Grand Prix — Risk Register

Seeded from the seven technical challenges in the GDD (§12) plus three
production/market risks specific to a single-mechanic, premium, offline
title. Likelihood and Impact are rated Low / Medium / High; Score is the
product on a 1–3 scale (Low=1, Medium=2, High=3), so 9 is the ceiling.

| ID | Risk | Category | Likelihood | Impact | Score | Mitigation | Owner | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | Sensor variance across OEMs — Android gyro fusion quality, sample rate, and axis conventions differ wildly by vendor; some report at 20 Hz | Technical | High | High | 9 | Abstract all sensor reads behind one calibration layer (`Input/MotionController.swift` already does this for iOS). Resample to a fixed internal rate with interpolation. Ship a per-device profile table as data, updatable without a client release. Android launch is deliberately held 6 months post-iOS specifically to build this table from real field data. | Gameplay engineer | QA device-matrix pass (M10) surfaces >2 devices with unusable tilt response |
| R-02 | Drift and rest-pose loss — long runs and body movement shift the neutral pose; the car feels like it's "pulling" | Technical | High | High | 9 | Rest pose is relative and re-centrable with a single tap (`GameSession.recentre()`). Vehicle Mode applies a 4-second rolling average (`Tuning.vehicleModeWindow`). Telemetry flags runs with more than two re-centres; those courses get retuned. | Gameplay engineer | Telemetry shows >15% of runs on a course include 2+ re-centres |
| R-03 | Parallax vs. motion sickness — a view that shifts with tilt is exactly the stimulus that provokes discomfort in a minority of players | Technical | Medium | Medium | 4 | Parallax depth is a 0–100% slider (`Persistence.parallaxDepth`) fully decoupled from physics, so reducing it never changes handling or recorded times. Default 100%, prompted at first launch. Leaderboard-legal at any setting. | Graphics engineer | App Store review or beta feedback flags nausea/discomfort reports |
| R-04 | Fill-rate on the layered diorama — carpet, shadow, posts, underside, deck, dashes, boost overlay, two rails, car, ghost is 9+ overdrawn layers at up to 120 fps on a small GPU | Technical | Medium | Medium | 4 | Cull to the visible arc only (`Tuning.visibleAhead`/`visibleBehind`, ~62 samples). Bake the carpet to a tileable procedural texture drawn as one full-screen triangle. Batch each layer into one static mesh, drawn as a single contiguous vertex range (eight draws per frame, no per-frame geometry). Already proven at 60 fps on iPhone 12 in the HTML prototype. | Graphics engineer | Frame-pacing telemetry on minimum-spec device drops below 55 fps sustained |
| R-05 | Deterministic ghosts — a replay that doesn't match across devices and app versions makes every stored best time a lie | Technical | Medium | Medium | 4 | Fixed 120 Hz physics tick (`Tuning.physicsHz`) fully decoupled from render rate. Ghosts store sampled *position*, not input (`GhostFrame`), so a later tuning change never invalidates a stored time — the ghost still draws the line that was actually driven. Raw tilt is kept alongside for display only. | Gameplay engineer | A tuning-constant change causes a stored ghost to visibly clip a rail or miss a checkpoint on replay |
| R-06 | Audio Course Mode fidelity — a stereo field must convey lane position, rail proximity, and grade simultaneously without becoming noise | Technical | Medium | Medium | 4 | Three separate, independently mixable channels (pan / tick-rate / pitch), with a dedicated audio-designer pass at M8 and an independent accessibility audit at M11. Ship an honest per-course playability list (14 of 38) rather than a blanket claim. | Composer + QA | Accessibility audit at M11 rates any of the 14 committed courses as not honestly playable by sound alone |
| R-07 | Native permission model — `CMMotionManager` requires the user to grant Motion & Fitness access, and the browser-based prototype separately requires a secure context and an explicit gesture | Technical | Low | Low | 1 | Native app gates behind a clear, contextual permission prompt with a fallback to touch-drag / arrow-key control if denied (`FallbackController`), so the run is never blocked, only flagged in the ghost record. The HTML prototype (marketing/demo use only) already handles the equivalent web permission gate. | Gameplay engineer | Permission-denial rate in analytics exceeds 5% of first sessions |
| R-08 | Premium-price conversion risk — a $4.99 upfront ask against a library of free-to-play competitors may suppress conversion below the 5–7% premium-mobile-demo benchmark the plan assumes | Market | Medium | High | 6 | Free 4-course demo (Playroom) carries the entire conversion load and is the only pre-purchase proof the mechanic works. No launch discount, which would train the audience to wait. KPI target is ≥5.0% demo→paid; Floor scenario in the GDD assumes only 3.5% and still models a viable (if thin) outcome. | Producer | Demo→paid conversion tracks below 3.5% for 30 consecutive days post-launch |
| R-09 | Single-mechanic risk — the entire game is one input (tilt-as-gravity) driving one core loop; if it does not land, there is no fallback game underneath it | Production | Low | High | 6 | This is precisely what the M3 feel-lock gate exists to catch, before six more months of content are built on top of an unproven premise. A failed gate is not treated as a launch risk to manage around — it triggers an explicit scope cut and budget rebuild (see `production/milestone-schedule.md`), not a "fix it later" note. | Technical director | M3 external test panel returns under 70% unprompted "natural" response |
| R-10 | Apple feature dependency in the breakout case — the plan's Breakout revenue scenario ($1,006,040 net) explicitly assumes an Apple editorial feature ("Games We Love") plus one viral demo video; neither is something the team controls or can schedule | Market | Medium | Medium | 4 | Marketing budget ($85,000) is weighted specifically to producing the single most screen-recordable asset in the game — the parallax effect itself — to maximize the odds of organic pickup, but Apple featuring is not solicited or guaranteed by any action in this plan. The budget in `production/budget-breakdown.md` is built to close on the Good scenario, not the Breakout one, so the business does not depend on this risk resolving favorably. | Producer | N/A — tracked as a standing assumption, not a triggerable event; revisit at each launch-window planning pass |

## Reading the scores

R-01 and R-02 (sensor variance, drift) are the only two risks at the ceiling
score of 9, and they are the same risk from two angles: the game's entire
input model is a raw hardware signal with no engine-provided abstraction
layer to absorb vendor differences. Both are owned by the same engineer for
that reason. R-08 and R-09 are the two risks that can kill the business
outright rather than degrade it — a soft conversion rate or a mechanic that
doesn't read as natural — and both have an explicit, pre-committed response
(cut scope, don't push through) rather than a wait-and-see mitigation.
