# Carpet Grand Prix — Bit

**Class:** Micro
**Theme:** Curiosity
**Handling identity:** Half-size hitbox; can take lines nothing else fits
**Unlock:** Basement complete

## Character

Bit is the smallest car in the roster by a wide margin, and its handling identity is
built entirely around what that smallness makes possible rather than what it costs.
Every other handling class trades one stat against another — mass against grip,
speed against rail tolerance. Bit doesn't trade; it *fits*. Gaps that read as solid
wall to the other seven cars are open road to Bit, and the whole of Bit's skill
ceiling lives in a player learning to see a course not as the track the level artist
drew, but as the track the level artist drew plus every seam, gap, and rafter space
Bit alone can use.

Its visual tic is a headlight flicker that lands a half-beat ahead of every other
car's — Bit's lights seem to already be looking at whatever's coming next, a half-step
ahead of the moment the rest of the roster reacts to it. It is a small, easy-to-miss
detail, matching the character: Bit is not a car that announces itself.

## Handling as theme

Curiosity, mechanically, is the reward for looking somewhere nobody told you to
look. Bit's half-size hitbox is a literal, mechanical answer to the question "what
happens if you go where you're not supposed to fit" — and the level design rewards
that instinct directly, because several of the harder courses in the Attic and
Basement have geometry (rafter gaps, narrow drops) that exists specifically so Bit
has somewhere the rest of the roster does not. Driving Bit well means treating every
course as a question rather than a fixed shape.

## Role in the story beats

Bit's moment in the Rising Action vignette is the clearest single-shot statement of
character in the whole narrative: Bit squeezes a strip of stolen track through a gap
under a closed door, a gap none of the larger cars in the sequence could have used,
and the entire line of cars waiting behind that door surges through the opening Bit
just made. It is not framed as heroics. It is framed as simply doing what Bit does —
finding the way through that nobody else could see was there, and then holding the
door, functionally, for everyone else.

## How the player should feel

Delight, specifically the delight of finding something the game didn't spell out for
you. Bit is the roster's reward for exploration and for looking twice at a course
that felt fully solved — it should make a player who's been playing the same layout
for an hour suddenly see three new lines they hadn't noticed, and feel, briefly,
exactly as curious as the car they're driving.
