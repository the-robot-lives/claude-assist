# Carpet Grand Prix — Red 7

**Class:** Muscle
**Theme:** Loyalty
**Handling identity:** Baseline. Balanced mass, honest grip. The starter.
**Unlock:** Available from the first run.

## Character

Red 7 is the car the kid actually played with. Every other car in the roster is
introduced by the story after the fact — waking up, being stolen back, being
discovered in the box — but Red 7 is on screen before the story starts, in the
player's hand, in Vignette 1's opening shot, pushed down the couch-cushion ramp by
the kid's own hand. It is the only car with a scar of continuity: a chipped paint
scar on the left rear fender that is present from that very first frame and never
retouched, in any vignette, at any point across the whole nine-part arc.

Red 7 does not lead the pack because it is the fastest or the strongest — on paper
it is the most average car in the game, deliberately. It leads because it is first,
and because it is the one the story keeps returning its camera to. When the full
roster crosses the threshold into the rain in the Climax vignette, Red 7 is framed
centre. When the story closes on an empty loop of new track in the final shot, it is
Red 7 — not any other car — that rolls into frame on its own and takes the first lap.

## Handling as theme

Balanced mass and honest grip mean Red 7 rewards no particular playstyle and
punishes no particular mistake more than any other car would. It is not the game's
best car on any single room's leaderboard once players have unlocked specialists —
The Loaf will out-grade it on the Attic, Wasp will out-run it on the Hallway
straights — but it is never the *wrong* car anywhere, and it is the only car that can
legitimately finish gold in all seven rooms without the player ever needing to think
about matchups. That reliability, not raw performance, is the whole of its handling
identity, and it is the same quality the theme word "Loyalty" is naming: Red 7 shows
up, everywhere, competently, without being asked twice.

## Role in the story beats

Red 7 is present, visibly, in every one of the nine vignettes — the only car with
that distinction. It is the car in the kid's hand at Equilibrium. It is the first to
twitch awake in the First Complication. It is framed at the head of the pack in the
Climax. And it is the one that closes the loop in the final shot of Resolution Part
Two, taking the first lap of track laid for a kid the player has never seen the face
of. Nothing about that closing beat is explained. It does not need to be — the player
has spent the entire game learning that this is simply what Red 7 does.

## How the player should feel

Fondness without spectacle. Red 7 is meant to be the car a player's hand goes to by
habit rather than by strategy — the equivalent of a house key you stop consciously
noticing you're carrying. By the time the credits vignette plays, Red 7 should feel
less like a vehicle with stats and more like a witness: the one constant across a
story about a house full of things that are, one by one, either lost or passed on.
