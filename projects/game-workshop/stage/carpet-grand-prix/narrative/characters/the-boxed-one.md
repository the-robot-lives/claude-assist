# Carpet Grand Prix — The Boxed One

**Class:** Unknown
**Theme:** Loss
**Handling identity:** Locked. Recovered in the Garden finale. Perfect stats.
**Unlock:** Story complete

## Character

The Boxed One is the car that got swept into the donation box in the Inciting
Incident and never woke up with the rest of the roster in the First Complication.
It is not one of the eight cars the player starts building a relationship with — it
is the absence those eight cars spend the whole rising action chasing, indirectly,
without the player being told that's what's happening. It first appears, as a
silhouette only, in the Climax vignette: a ninth shape on the garden path, up ahead
in the rain, rendered with no livery, no paint detail, no seams — a near-black
outline with the same body proportions as Red 7, but none of Red 7's identity filled
in.

That absence of detail is not a placeholder; it is the point. The Boxed One is what
a die-cast car looks like when nobody has played with it long enough to chip its
paint. Every other car in the roster earned its visual tic through use — a scar, a
glint, a flicker. The Boxed One has none, because it never got the time.

## Handling as theme

Once unlocked, The Boxed One is mechanically the outlier of the roster: it does not
sit on the mass-versus-grip trade-off curve every other handling class is built
from. It simply performs — full mass authority, full grip, no compromise. That is
a deliberate, slightly uncomfortable design choice: the "best" car in the game,
statistically, is the one defined entirely by loss, and the game does not soften
that irony or explain it away. It is not a power fantasy reward. It is closer to
what the game is quietly saying about the cost of the story that led here.

## Role in the story beats

The Boxed One exists structurally as the object of the Climax's chase and the
subject of the two-part Resolution: Vignette 8 shows the box it came from, opened
and undisturbed, its livery-less silhouette one of several cars glimpsed inside; the
handling-class unlock itself only completes after the player has finished the whole
story, meaning The Boxed One is the one car in the roster the player cannot actually
drive until they already know exactly what it cost the story to get it back.

## How the player should feel

Quiet, and a little unsettled by how good it feels to drive. The design intent is
for a player's first lap in The Boxed One to be simultaneously the most mechanically
satisfying car in the game and the one that sits worst in the stomach — because by
the time they've earned it, they know precisely what "recovered" means for a car
that was, for most of the story, simply gone.
