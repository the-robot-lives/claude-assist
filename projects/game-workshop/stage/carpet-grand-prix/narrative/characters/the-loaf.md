# Carpet Grand Prix — The Loaf

**Class:** Van
**Theme:** Stubbornness
**Handling identity:** Heaviest; enormous gravity authority, no grip at all
**Unlock:** Kitchen complete

## Character

The Loaf is a die-cast van, and it drives like one — all mass, no manners. Where
Red 7 is the game's constant and Chrome is its vanity, The Loaf is its stubbornness:
a car built entirely around the idea that momentum, applied long enough, is its own
argument. It does not corner so much as insist on a direction and wait for the
track to agree with it. On steep grades — the Attic, the Basement — that stubbornness
is exactly what wins races: nothing else in the roster pulls harder on a downhill
tilt. On tight technical courses it is exactly what loses them, because The Loaf
physically cannot apologize its way around a hairpin the way Chrome or Bit can.

Its one visual tic carries the theme through into idle animation: The Loaf never
fully stops the instant it crosses a finish line or checkpoint gate. It rolls a
visible extra foot past the line before settling — every single time, whether the
run was a personal best or a disaster. It doesn't know how to quit cleanly. It just
eventually runs out of momentum.

## Handling as theme

The tuning model ties mass directly to gravity authority and inversely to grip —
The Loaf sits at the far heavy end of that curve, the counterweight to Chrome at the
far light end. Playing The Loaf well means accepting that you cannot out-steer a
mistake; you can only out-commit to the line you picked before you made it. That is
stubbornness as a piece of mechanical feel rather than a stated trait: the player
learns what stubbornness costs and buys by learning what it feels like to drive a
vehicle that has already made up its mind.

## Role in the story beats

The Loaf is the first car singled out by name-equivalent action in the Rising
Action vignette — alone, dragging a single straight section of track out from under
the donation box's flap, a task the vignette gives the full three of its thirteen
seconds to, because The Loaf does nothing quickly. Where Bit later opens a path by
fitting through a gap nothing else can, The Loaf opens a path earlier in the same
beat by simple, unglamorous, sustained effort — the two cars are a deliberate pair,
showing two different ways of solving the same problem (the track buried under the
box), one through cleverness and one through will.

## How the player should feel

Respect, and a little bit of exasperated affection — the same feeling you have for
a person who is completely unpersuadable but who you'd want on your side of an
argument. The Loaf is never the elegant choice. It is, race after race on the
steepest rooms, the choice that gets the job done anyway, on its own terms, at its
own pace, a beat behind everyone else and somehow still first.
