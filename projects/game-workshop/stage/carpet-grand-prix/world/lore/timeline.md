# Carpet Grand Prix — Timeline

Carpet Grand Prix has no calendar. Every in-fiction event in this document is dated
relative to **Night 1** — the night the cars first move under their own power, the
central pivot the entire story hangs from — never to a real-world year. This is
consistent with the world bible's rule that no magic is ever explained: the game
never confirms how long any of this actually took, only the order it happened in
and, in a few cases, roughly how far apart.

## Before Night 1

### Years Before Night 1 — The Kid Plays

The track set is a gift, new, unremarkable — the ordinary way a kid ends up with a
box of die-cast cars and a length of orange plastic track. Across several years,
told entirely through the implied wear on the track and the roster's own visual
tics (Red 7's chipped fender is the one piece of evidence the game ever shows), the
set gets built, rebuilt, and raced across a couch cushion and a rug, over and over,
the way a favourite toy does. None of this is shown on screen. It is the unshown
years the Equilibrium vignette is the last frame of.

### The Last Evening — Equilibrium

The evening before Night 1. Dusk. A kid's hand pushes Red 7 down the couch-cushion
ramp one final time, the way it's been done for years, except this time the hand
withdraws and does not come back. Nothing marks this evening as different from any
other evening that came before it — that's the point. Nobody in the fiction knows
yet that it's the last one.

### The Next Morning — The Donation Box

The morning after the Last Evening. A cardboard box arrives on the playroom floor
and is labelled DONATE in marker. An adult sweeps roughly half the track into it
within the hour. The cars — still ordinary toys, not yet animate — are left where
the kid set them down the night before, untouched by the sweep, facing a box that
has just taken away most of what they need to run.

## Night 1 — The Cars Wake

The night of the same day the box arrived. For the first time, the cars move under
their own power — badly, testing the idea of motion the way a newborn animal tests
its legs. Four of the eight handling-class cars wake this first night: Red 7 first,
then Chrome, The Loaf, and Wasp. They race, unsteadily, on whatever half of the
track the morning's sweep left behind.

## Night 2 through Night 4 — Rising Action

Across these three nights, the remaining cars wake (Rustbucket, Sparks, Bit) and the
whole roster begins dragging reclaimed track pieces out from under the donation
box's flap and back into use, room by room. By the end of Night 4, the reclaimed
circuit has grown from the playroom rug alone into a line that crosses under a
doorway and partway into the hallway — the first night the house itself, not just a
single room, functions as a track.

## Night 5 — The 2 a.m. Encounter

The kid comes downstairs at roughly 2 a.m. for water and finds track laid across the
kitchen tile, the cars frozen mid-line the instant the stairs creaked. A long pause.
The kid steps over the track rather than through it, continues to the sink, and goes
back to bed without ever mentioning what they saw — not that night, and not at any
point afterward shown anywhere else in the story. This is the story's midpoint, both
narratively and, roughly, chronologically — Night 5 of what the timeline eventually
totals to seven.

## Night 6 — The Crisis

The donation box, still on the playroom floor with whatever track was never
reclaimed still inside it, is taped shut and carried out of the house to the garage.
The remaining cars — cut off from that portion of the circuit permanently — can now
only reach the basement, the steepest and darkest run available to them. Sparks
noses forward first into the basement stairwell, alone, ahead of the others.

## Night 7 — The Climax

The full team — all eight handling-class cars, together for the only time in the
story — cross out the back door and down the garden path in the rain, chasing a
ninth silhouette glimpsed ahead of them on the path: The Boxed One, the one car that
never got the chance to wake on Night 1 because it was already gone, inside the
sealed box, by the time the others found their legs. The chase ends at the low
garden wall marking the edge of the playable world. What happens exactly at the
wall is not shown.

## Some Time After Night 7 — The Box Reopens

No count of nights is ever given for this gap, deliberately — the world bible's "no
magic explained" rule extends to time itself here. The donation box turns up open on
the sunlit patio, undisturbed for what the light and the box's faded marker ink
imply has been a long stretch. Every piece of track and every car that never got the
chance to wake is still inside it, untouched. Nothing was thrown away.

## Later Still — The New Track

The final beat of the story is not dated relative to Night 7 at all, deliberately —
it is set apart, at an unspecified later point, signalled only by a visibly older,
taller figure kneeling on the lawn to lay a short, simple loop of brand-new track,
not track pulled from the donate box, for a second, smaller figure standing behind
them. The loop is laid in the same warm late light as the Last Evening, the only two
points in the entire timeline that share that exact light signature. Red 7 rolls
into frame on its own and takes the first lap. The story ends where it began,
inverted, with no further night ever numbered.
