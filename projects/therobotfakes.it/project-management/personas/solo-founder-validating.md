# Maya Okafor — Solo Founder / Indie Hacker

**Tagline:** "I need to *show* the idea this weekend, not spend three weeks building it."

## Demographics / context
- 31, technical-ish founder; can read code, ships with no-code + a little React.
- Bootstrapping her second micro-SaaS from a coworking desk and nights/weekends.
- Time-boxed: validates an idea in days, kills it fast if nobody bites.
- Audience for her prototypes: 5–10 potential customers on calls, plus a waitlist landing page.

## Goals
- Go from a vague idea to a clickable, believable demo in a single sitting.
- Run the demo in front of real users and watch where they get confused.
- Reuse the winning prototype as the *actual* starting code, not throwaway art.
- Avoid committing engineering time before there's a signal of demand.

## Frustrations / pain points with current tools
- Figma demos look real but produce zero code — she rebuilds everything anyway.
- Balsamiq/Excalidraw sketches are fast but die the moment she opens an editor.
- Hand-coding a prototype eats the whole weekend on plumbing, not the idea.
- Restyling a hand-rolled prototype to look "investor-ready" is a slog.

## How she'd use TRFI specifically
- Types screens in the **text DSL** during a discovery call — login, dashboard, the one magic screen — in minutes.
- Keeps everything **low-fi** while she's still arguing with herself about structure.
- Drops a 12-value **theme.yaml**, flips **high-fi**, and suddenly has a pitch-worthy mockup with no rework.
- Wires one **FSM + API/LLM** path so the "magic" screen actually returns a real result on the call.
- When an idea survives, **exports Lit/HTML** and starts building on top of the same artifact.

## Key features she cares about
- **Text DSL** — fastest possible authoring, no mouse.
- **Low-fi/high-fi toggle** — one switch from sketch to demo, zero redraw.
- **theme.yaml** — believable polish from a handful of seed tokens.
- **FSM + LLM/API** — a fake that behaves real enough to test the value prop.
- **Lit/HTML export** — the prototype becomes the build, not landfill.

## Representative quote
> "Every other tool makes me choose between fast-and-fake or slow-and-real. I want the fake to *turn into* the real one."

## Success looks like
She validates or kills an idea in a weekend, and the surviving prototype is the first commit of the product — not a screenshot she throws away.
